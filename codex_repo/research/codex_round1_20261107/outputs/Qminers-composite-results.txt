HOW THE APPROACHES ARE COMBINED
1. Queue preservation and replenishment: retain live same-price quotes and refill available capacity behind them, instead of losing priority through repeated replacements.
2. Depletion protection: pull a side when external volume at its best price falls below 20 lots; step the inventory-increasing quote one tick back when inventory error reaches 95 lots.
3. Causal forecasting: a regularized linear model combines spread, touch/depth imbalance, signed flow, short price trends, displayed depth, flow volume, and microprice displacement. Features update every 100 ms; flow updates on each event.
4. Forecast-driven inventory: target inventory is 40 times the predicted one-second price change, clipped to ±50 lots. Suppress a quote when its forecast edge is below 0.15 ticks.
5. Strict reservation: position plus all pending buys stays at or below 100, and position minus all pending sells stays at or above −100. Cancelling orders retain their reservation until acknowledged; buys and sells are never netted against each other.
6. Session control: stop adding to an existing position in the last 30 seconds of the configured 5,750-second session. This is passive wind-down, not guaranteed flattening; the final position was −36 and the official score includes its liquidation value.

MODEL COMPARISON
The tested ensemble combined 76.3% one-second Ridge forecast and 23.7% shallow gradient-boosted trees. Five-second Ridge, raw microprice, and the zero forecast received effectively zero blend weight. The blend slightly improved validation forecast correlation (0.4055 versus 0.4035 for Ridge), and policy validation PnL (69,257 versus 69,053), but its full PnL was 4,243 lower. The delivered maximum-PnL file uses the linear forecast only within the composite execution policy. Unused ensemble constants are retained to preserve the exact tested export. Increasing complexity did not win this comparison.

SELECTION AND EVIDENCE LIMITS
Models were first fitted on 0–40% of the tape; nonnegative blend weights were calibrated on 40–50%; the base models were then refitted on the first 50%. Labels crossing split boundaries were purged. Thirty-seven policy trials used the 50–75% segment for selection, with every replay starting at the beginning to preserve the order book and FIFO queues. The ensemble policy won that validation comparison and was frozen before the full runs. Its matched linear-only alternative was frozen at the same time. The linear version subsequently earned the highest full-tape PnL. Calling it the highest observed public-data candidate therefore uses the full results for selection. The last quarter had already been inspected in earlier work and team research; it is a reused diagnostic segment, not an untouched holdout. Full PnL also includes the model training interval.
