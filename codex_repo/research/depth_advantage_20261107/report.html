Architecture

1 · Market feed
Market-only bid/ask and individual orders; aggressive trade flow.
2 · Features
13 supplied signals; 3 new signed nonlinear depth terms.
3 · Causal learner
Mature 100 ms labels → decayed ridge statistics → fit each second.
4 · Fair price
Midpoint + clipped forecast − inventory skew.
5 · Execution
Passive quotes, queue preservation and strict reservation ledger.

Observations are sampled at most once per 100 ms. Each becomes a label at the first usable callback at or after its 100 ms horizon. Labels delayed by more than one extra second are discarded. After 200 matured labels, roughly 20 seconds, the learned forecast replaces a microprice-style fallback; the bot can quote during warm-up.

The learner maintains exponentially weighted XᵀX and Xᵀy, with a 300-second e-folding memory (about 208-second half-life). It solves (XᵀWX + λI)β = XᵀWy once per second. Ridge is max(10, 0.001 × effective mass), so the floor usually dominates at this sampling rate. The prediction is clipped to ±2 ticks. This is regularized online regression with nonlinear basis functions, not a neural network.

Per-order size is capped at 100 when constructing robust depth signals. Order counts and capped sizes give a different view from raw summed volume. They reduce dependence on a single large order, but order fragmentation can still change them. “Two ticks” means a price-distance window, not exactly two populated levels.

prediction = clip(dot(weights, features), -2, +2)
fair = mid + prediction - 0.05 × inventory / 100 × max(1, spread)
if |fair - mid| ≤ 0.1: fair = mid
buy_price  = min(best_bid, floor(fair - 0.5))
sell_price = max(best_ask, ceil(fair + 0.5))

Each side can have up to eight orders. Existing same-price orders retain queue position; newly available capacity is replenished. Pending submissions and cancels retain their remaining exposure reservation. A 200-lot order is possible when moving from −100 to +100, while each side independently stays within the ±100 bound. Prices are passive when chosen; latency can change the book before activation.

The flow guard is inactive. Flow is net signed volume divided by 1 + total volume, so its absolute value is below 1. The inherited ±1.1 thresholds never fire. Inventory-limit checks are active. This experiment keeps that behavior; turning the guard on would be a separate execution change.

What the tests established

The supplied attachment is AST-identical to the repository’s 523,999 strategy. Its header incorrectly said five features. Full replay reproduced the recorded score. Removing unused queue-ahead bookkeeping left the entire PnL curve, official result, learned weights and order-status counts identical. In the paired full runs, the cleanup used 16.5% less CPU.

The controlled ablations keep execution and other parameters fixed. Their screening results show that robust depth explains a material part of the gain. The added signed nonlinear depth terms improved both screening halves and 7 of 10 time blocks. A 100/500 ms horizon ensemble improved the total but lost in the first half, so it was rejected under the frozen rule. The fast/slow memory blend and recent-depth-change features also failed.
